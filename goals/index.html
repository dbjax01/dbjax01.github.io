Career goals

I want to teach business and financial literacy at the secondary level and help students discover the same kind of purpose and opportunity that my teachers helped me find.

I have wanted to be a teacher since fourth grade, when my elementary teachers, including Ms. Davis and Ms. Brown, showed me how much of a difference a teacher can make in a student’s life. I did not know what I wanted to teach until I took a business class in high school. My teacher, Mr. Kelly, introduced me to a subject I came to love: business and financial literacy. That experience led me to pursue degrees in both business and education at the University of Florida.

After graduating, I began teaching technology and business courses at Kanapaha Middle School. Since then, I have also taught in a juvenile justice program and now serve in the Peace Corps, where I teach financial education in Peru. Each experience has given me a different perspective on teaching and helped me better understand how to reach students with different backgrounds, needs, and goals.

I want to bring those experiences into a secondary classroom where students learn skills they can use long after graduation: how to budget, save, make informed borrowing decisions, and turn an idea into something of their own.

Why financial literacy matters

Financial decisions shape nearly every part of adult life. They influence where we live, the opportunities available to us, the stress we carry, and the choices we can make for our families. Yet many students leave school without ever learning how to build a budget, understand credit, compare loan offers, or plan for a major purchase.

I believe financial education is one of the most practical ways schools can expand opportunity. Students shouldn’t have to learn these lessons entirely through trial and error. They deserve the knowledge and confidence to make informed decisions before those decisions carry real consequences.

The need is becoming even greater as the cost of living changes and technology reshapes the workplace. I want students to understand not only how money works, but also how to adapt, recognize opportunities, and make decisions about their futures.

The program I want to build

When I return from the Peace Corps, I want to build a business and financial education program that students are excited to be part of.

I envision a classroom where students learn by doing. They build budgets around careers they are interested in, compare actual borrowing options, manage investment portfolios, and develop business ideas from initial research through a final presentation. Technology is a tool they use to solve problems, analyze information, and communicate their ideas.

I also want students to have opportunities beyond the classroom. I would love to sponsor DECA or FBLA, helping students develop their business skills through competitions, leadership opportunities, and collaboration with other students and professionals.

Most importantly, I want to build a program that responds to the students and community it serves. That means getting to know my students, collaborating with other teachers, listening to local needs, and creating opportunities that connect what students learn to the world around them.
