Career goals

I want to teach business and financial literacy in a secondary school, and to be the kind of teacher who helped me.

I have wanted to be a teacher since the 4th grade, when my elementary teachers, including Ms. Davis and Ms. Brown, showed me what a teacher could be for a student. I did not know what I wanted to teach until a high school business class. My teacher, Mr. Kelly, helped me discover a passion for business and financial literacy, and I went on to earn degrees in both business and education at the University of Florida.

After graduating, I was fortunate to be offered a job teaching technology and business courses at Kanapaha Middle School. Since then I have taught in a juvenile justice program and now serve in the Peace Corps, teaching financial education in Peru. Each setting taught me something different about how to reach students.

Next, I want a secondary classroom where students learn to budget, save, borrow wisely, and start something of their own.

Why financial literacy matters

The numbers explain why. About 71% of Americans say money is a significant cause of stress in their lives, and nearly 40% do not have the savings to cover an unexpected $400 expense. Money strains relationships too: in one study of divorced adults, about 37% named financial stress as a major contributing factor. Prices are 36% higher than they were ten years ago, and AI is changing what careers will look like. Students need to understand how our financial system works and how to plan for their future before they are on their own.

Sources: American Psychological Association; Federal Reserve; Scott, Rhoades, Stanley, Markman & Whitton (2013), Couple and Family Psychology; Bureau of Labor Statistics.

What I want to build

When I return from the Peace Corps, I want to build a business program that students are excited about. It will be project-based and focused on practical skills. Students will build a real budget, compare real loan offers, and take a small business idea from pitch to plan. I want to collaborate with other teachers, learn what matters most to my students, and shape my instruction around it.

I would love to teach entrepreneurship, business technology, finance, or accounting, and to sponsor DECA or FBLA. I am also interested in Family and Consumer Sciences and statistics, though I am not yet certified in those subjects. My work at Kanapaha Middle School, in Peru, and on Flo-Fly taught me that students learn money skills best by practicing them.
