Kanapaha Middle School and Virtual Enterprises International

As a CTE teacher in Alachua County Public Schools (August 2023 to May 2024), I built and taught financial and technological literacy courses for students in grades 6-8.

Grades 6-8Courses 4Certifications earned 150+Projects 47

What I was responsible for

Four courses, start to finish

I wrote the syllabi, lessons and projects for Computer Applications in Business 1 and 2 and ICT Essentials 1 and 2, all aligned to the Florida CTE curriculum frameworks.

Certification prep

I prepared students for Digital Tools certification exams with test prep and practice tests, and students earned more than 150 certifications across the year.

Weekly projects, daily practice

Students finished one or two projects every week and typed for a few minutes as bell work while I took attendance. Lessons were built in Nearpod.

Clear grading and a respectful room

Grades were point based and entered in Skyward weekly. The classroom ran on the Golden Rule and six non-negotiables, with routines posted so students always knew what to expect.

Inside my lessons

I built every lesson in Nearpod so each student answered, matched, drew or labeled something instead of just listening. A typical lesson starts with short teaching slides, checks understanding with an interactive question, and ends with something hands on.

In Computing Essentials, that meant labeling real computer parts at a table. In PCPartPicker, every table got a different budget, from $600 to $2,000, and the build had to be complete and compatible.

Preparing students for what comes next

Beyond the software, I wanted every student to leave with practical skills for high school and for life, including how to handle money.

Budgeting

Students built a budget around the career they most wanted, the place they wanted to live and the car they wanted to own, using the research and Excel skills they had built all year.

Money in practice

Students learned about credit scores and credit cards, traded a $100,000 paper portfolio all year, and had to write a check for every supply in the Tallest Tower challenge.

Career exploration

Students mapped possible lives, researched careers with the Bureau of Labor Statistics, learned about Florida colleges and Bright Futures, and found local volunteer organizations.

High school prep

8th graders looked at the electives at their prospective high school and picked classes that fit their interests.

Typing

Every class opened with five minutes of typing practice on typing.com.

Professional writing

Students practiced writing professional emails, then wrote a Future Email scheduled to reach them years later.

SMART goals

Students learned what makes a goal specific and measurable, then set goals of their own.

Responsible AI

Students learned how to use AI responsibly, alongside lessons on digital wellbeing.

Capstone: a digital portfolio

Students used Google Sites to build an e-portfolio showcasing their projects and what they learned during the semester or year.

How I designed the projects

  • Teach to the test where it matters. The questions on certification test prep closely match the real exam, so I pushed students to finish them.
  • Let students see their growth. Practice tests came up periodically so students could watch their scores climb.
  • Build one skill on the next. Each Excel assignment added to the last, and the unit ended with a payroll register that used everything students had learned.

Virtual Enterprises International

Virtual Enterprises International (VEI) is a nonprofit based in New York that promotes entrepreneurship and digital literacy in the classroom. Its Junior Ventures program, VE-JV, is VEI’s middle school program, and it was brand new when I started teaching.

VEI provides resources for teachers and hosts a professional learning conference in Brooklyn, which I attended.

Students are introduced to Technology Applications, where they engage in typical business projects using spreadsheet, presentation, word-processing, graphics, and web design software. Activities are centered around an entrepreneurial theme and include projects that allow students to develop foundational skills in business and technology.

In my classes, that showed up in projects like designing a VE-JV poster in Word and entering the VEI Dream Job Essay Competition.
